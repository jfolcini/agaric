## Session 3993 — sync says why: a device that is away, and two versions that cannot sync

`Refs #6523` (two of four items; a forked history and an engine/SQL disagreement stay open).

- A paired device that is simply away reads "Waiting for <device>, last synced <when>", with a grey
  dot and no toast. Away is a dial timeout to a paired peer with no mDNS announcement and no recent
  pull from it, coded `peer_away`.
- An engine-format mismatch is explained on both devices and names the one to update. The device
  that was dialled answers the handshake with a fixed refusal sentence worded for the dialler,
  which maps it back to `engine_format_mismatch` with `peer_is_older`. An older peer that closes
  right after the handshake, having synced before, is coded `handshake_unanswered`.

Decisions and traps:

- `peer_away` repeats on every attempt. A withheld repeat ends the attempt's progress channel in a
  bare error, which the frontend reads as idle on a scheduled tick and as a failure on a press
  (#6601), so reporting once per streak would drop the waiting line within a minute. It has its own
  log text: repeats are suppressed by matching text, and sharing the timeout text would swallow the
  first real timeout once the peer is back.
- A peer still pulling from us as initiator is not away, and neither is a peer with no row: the host
  a pairing window scanned on a LAN without multicast would otherwise time out in silence.
- The "update both" wording is gone: every mismatch event carries `peer_is_older`.
- The dialler no longer adds a generic "Sync failed" after the version explanation; it used the
  same toast id and store field and overwrote it.
- A device that refuses on version stops there: it does not bind the peer or record its name.
- Existing Rust assertions changed on purpose: the refusal is now a frame, not a closed stream, and
  two connection-failure tests state that mDNS still announces the peer, because a silent peer now
  counts as away.
- A negative toast check passes before a toast can render. Add a later control toast and assert
  the exact list.

Gaps, raised with the maintainer:

- A `start_sync` refused because the away peer is in backoff, or mid-dial, still toasts "Sync failed
  for device" once per launch, from the scheduled tick as well as from a press.
- A device that has only just left reads as failed until its mDNS entry goes stale: its first
  timeout toasts, and a second one does once it stops counting as pulling from us.
- The status card's "Last synced" counts this session only, so it can show "--" beside the
  waiting line's real time.
- A build already shipped shows the new refusal sentence inside its own "Sync failed" wrapper.

Ran: the `agaric-sync` package and the app's sync tests, clippy over the workspace, the hooks,
stores, layout, agenda and peers vitest suites, typecheck, and the fourteen sync Playwright specs.
Both whole e2e lanes were left to CI.

Review ran clippy over the workspace, the full vitest, typecheck, lint, both prek stages, the
`@sync` spec files in `e2e/`, and the workspace nextest in two partitions on the first commit and
the first partition again after the review fix, whose own tests ran alone; timeouts under a load
average near 60, in tests the change does not touch, passed alone or on retry. The real-backend
two-device spec was left to CI: both of its devices run one build, so it cannot reach the mismatch.

## Session 3992 — buttons in Trash and History work from the keyboard

The same family as session 3984's Pages fix, confirmed by its reviewer; no issue.

Trash and History heard Enter and Space at the document and acted on them from any focused
control. Space on *Restore all*, *Empty trash*, the op-type legend or *Load more* selected the
list cursor's row, and while a History row was selected, Enter on the legend opened the revert
confirmation. Inside a History row, Space on *Reset to this point* or *Diff* selected the row
instead of pressing the button: the row's own handler caught the key on its way up.

One predicate, `pressesFocusedControl` beside `useListKeyboardNavigation`, now gates Pages, Trash
and History. Enter and Space belong to the list only when focus is on nothing in particular
(`<body>`, or an element that holds the list such as `#main-content`), on the list itself, or on
the row holding its Tab stop. Anywhere else the key is the focused control's. A History row now
toggles itself on Space only when the Space is pressed on the row.

Decisions and traps:

- "The list or the body" alone, as #6654 had it for Pages, breaks History, whose Tab stop and
  clicked rows hold focus for Enter-to-revert, and Trash, where focus after a view switch is
  `#main-content`.
- "Any row", the first draft, broke Pages: a click in a row's padding focuses the row
  (`tabindex="-1"`) without moving the cursor, and Enter then opened the cursor's page. Only a
  row with `tabindex="0"`, the roving Tab stop of History and Trash, counts.
- `BatchActionToolbar`'s `stopPropagation` shielded only the three document listeners, and its
  `tabIndex` existed only because a toolbar with a key handler must be focusable; both are
  deleted. Nothing focuses the toolbar.
- The checkbox `stopPropagation` calls stay (the row tests pin that a checkbox key stays off the
  document), as do the action and diff cells' (lint pairs them with the cells' `onClick`). In
  Trash rows they are what keeps the row's own Enter and Space handler off its buttons.
- `listRef` is required in `useTrashListShortcuts`; its hook test passes a null ref.
- No `target === document` arm: a real keydown targets the focused element or `<body>`. The
  History and Trash hook tests dispatch on `<body>`.

Ran: full vitest, typecheck, lint, `prek` (all files and manual stage), and the keyboard-controls,
Trash, History and Pages Playwright specs; each new spec shown red. The whole Playwright and
WebdriverIO lanes are CI's run.

## Session 3991 — delete and restore stop rebuilding the whole vault

`Refs #6522` (items 1 and 2; dropping the two indexes stays open).

- Delete and restore no longer enqueue `RebuildPageIds`. Their inputs to `page_id` and `space_id`
  (`parent_id`, `block_type`, the page's space) do not change, so the whole-vault rebuild could
  not change anything. Purge, inbound sync and the daily backstop keep it; a `needs_page_ids` flag
  on the lifecycle debounce makes one purge in a burst still fire it.
- `rebuild_space_ids` and `rebuild_pages_cache_counts` find the stale rows on the read pool and
  write only those. With nothing stale, the counts rebuild takes no write lock at all.

Decisions and traps:

- Both write halves re-derive under the lock from the ids the read phase found; they do not write
  the read phase's values. Create, edit and move maintain counts in their own transaction, so a
  value read earlier could overwrite a fresher one.
- The old one-statement forms stay as `#[cfg(test)]` oracles, and tests assert both agree. A test
  oracle that writes `blocks` outside `mod tests` trips the table-ownership baseline, so it lives
  inside the test module.
- A restore does write `page_id` in its own transaction, to the value the rebuild derives. The
  premise is about inputs, not writes.
- Delete and restore no longer repair page-id drift that already existed elsewhere in the vault,
  nor in the rows a batch restore or restore-all brings back: single restore and undo re-derive
  their subtree, the batch restore does not, and restore-all re-derives `page_id` but not
  `space_id`. That waits for the backstop, a purge or inbound sync. Raised with the maintainer.
- A seeded test drives creates, moves, space moves, deletes, single / batch / all restores, purges
  and undos through the commands and checks after every step that the full rebuild changes
  nothing; a longer search run of it found no drift either.
- The plan assertion reads the statement's SQL from the `query!` value, so it tests the production
  text and not a copy.
- Two mutations can mask each other: an unscoped write hides a read phase that misses rows.
  Falsification ran as two builds.

Not done: settled-time budgets and the before/after comparison, which needs `main`'s settled
time from a `bench-slo` run. The bench cannot show write-lock time: its materializer has no read
pool, so every rebuild runs its single-pool form, nothing contends for the lock during the flush,
and no tracing subscriber is installed, so a `slow BEGIN IMMEDIATE` warning could not appear in
its log.

Ran: the whole workspace under nextest, clippy over the workspace with all targets, the
`SQLX_OFFLINE` check, and both prek stages, on the final tree.

## Session 3990 — saved views are pages that sync

`Closes #6544` (last box). A saved view, of the Pages browser or of Advanced Query, was a
per-device preference. It is now a page of its space carrying the internal `saved_view` property,
whose value is the view (`v1:` plus base64url JSON), so views sync between devices and a deleted one
waits in the Trash. No migration, op type, table or store.

- **Hidden everywhere a page is listed.** One SQL fragment, `filters::NOT_SAVED_VIEW`, sits in every
  page listing, the Pages metadata query and its count, the three search scans, the advanced-query
  predicate, inline query blocks and `list_by_type`. By-id reads, by-property reads, the Trash,
  history and sync still see a view. `list_by_type` loses the `view_type` clause it replaced, which
  nothing wrote.
- **`NOT IN`, not `NOT EXISTS`.** `NOT EXISTS` changed two pinned query plans. `block_id` is
  `NOT NULL`, so `NOT IN` is safe, and SQLite answers it from `idx_block_props_key` once per query.
- **A view holds no page name.** `SpacePageNames` leaves views out, so a page can be created with,
  or renamed to, a view's title, and a restored view never collides. A view still cannot be renamed
  onto a page's title. Two views with one title, and a view named like a journal date, are refused
  by the frontend only.
- **A save is two writes.** `create_page_in_space`, then `set_property`. If the second fails the
  frontend trashes the page it made, so no empty page is left in the list.
- **Deleting a space.** Once views stopped counting as pages, a space holding only views read as
  empty but refused to delete. The delete now takes the views with it, in the same transaction; a
  space holding a real page is still refused, and the message counts real pages only.
- **Device views are moved once.** The views a device had saved become pages on first load of the
  space, then the old preference is cleared.

Two small follow-ups from earlier PRs of this sweep ride along: a failed bookmark write shows a
toast, and `template-utils` gained the tests its mutation report asked for.

Known limits, all in the PR body: views are not in the vault ZIP export or the Markdown mirror;
restoring a deleted space leaves its views in the Trash; a batch delete of a space id leaves the
space live in the engine, which predates this change and has no caller.

Checks run: `cargo check` and `cargo clippy` over the workspace with all targets, the sqlx cache
check, the whole nextest workspace in two partitions, vitest for the mock, `src/lib`, `src/hooks`
and the touched component folders, typecheck, lint, the manual-stage hooks, and the saved-views,
spaces, trash and history Playwright specs. Each exclusion and each step of the space delete was
shown red by breaking the code it covers. `e2e-tauri` and the benches that time these queries did
not run locally.

## Session 3989 — `agaric-mcp call <tool> <json>` runs one tool and exits

`Refs #6555` (the one-shot MCP call item).

The bridge binary was a byte shuttle with no MCP client of its own. `call` reuses its socket
lookup, `--token` and connect step, then runs rmcp's client over the stream: token preamble,
`ClientConfig::serve`, one `call_tool`. rmcp's `client` feature was already on, so there is no
hand-written JSON-RPC. The preamble now has one writer, `present_token`, shared by the bridge.

Decisions and traps:

- A refused token is an error to `initialize`; a tool error, including a write tool on a read-only
  token, is an error to the call. Both print the server's message on stderr and exit 1. Arguments
  that are not a JSON object exit 2 in `parse_args`, before anything connects.
- The result goes out through `writeln!`, not `println!`: `println!` panics when the reader closes
  the pipe early (`| head -c`), and release builds abort on panic. A failed write exits 1 naming it.
- The happy-path test runs `capture`, not the issue's `search`: `search` needs settled full-text
  indexing, and `capture` shows both the arguments going in and the stored block.
- `real_server` and `DEV` moved from `tests_rmcp.rs` into `mcp::test_support` so the binary smoke
  tests serve the real registries.
- `bridge_binary()` prefers `target/debug` over `target/release`: rebuild the debug binary after
  every change, or the smoke tests run a stale one.
- On Windows the release binary is a GUI-subsystem program, so `call` typed at an interactive
  console shows no output unless redirected. Not changed; nothing here builds Windows.

Ran: the `stub_binary_` smoke tests with `ci-smoke`, the binary's own tests, the `mcp::` tests, and
clippy over the workspace.

## Session 3985 — the editor-keyboard spec reads the caret after the browser reports it

Two tests in `e2e/editor-keyboard-6537.spec.ts` failed on PR CI (seen on #6648): "Shift+ArrowDown
past the end of block 2 selects blocks 2 and 3" and "ArrowUp from the second visual line of a
wrapped block stays in the block". One cause. Arrow keys inside a paragraph are handled by the
browser; ProseMirror learns where the caret went on the next `selectionchange`, which Chrome can
fire after the spec's next read. The spec's `caret()` helper read `editor.state` too early.

- In the first test a stale "not at end" read sent one Shift+ArrowDown too many. That press
  became block selection, which removes the editor, and the next read waited for an editor that
  was gone.
- In the second the read returned the offset from before the ArrowUp.

`caret()` now dispatches `selectionchange` before reading, as `selectEditorRange` in
`e2e/helpers.ts` does. No assertion changed and the product is unchanged: a person's next key
always comes after the event.

Traps:

- #6635 fixed the same mechanism in `block-keyboard-fundamentals.spec.ts`, not in this file.
- To reproduce, hold each real `selectionchange` until the next keydown or 300 ms: both tests
  then fail every run without the fix.
- A helper that reads `editor.state` straight after an arrow key is racy even with no further
  keys; sync first, or poll.

Ran: the file ten times at four and at eight workers under load, and `typecheck:e2e`.

## Session 3983 — a click right after a drag was being swallowed in specs

`e2e/page-link-drag.spec.ts` "a bookmark dropped on a block links the page at its end" failed on
PR CI with an empty `header-label` for fifteen seconds after clicking `Tags`. It was a lost click,
not a slow render. dnd-kit's sensors stop every click from the moment a drag starts until a 50 ms
timer after release (`AbstractPointerSensor.detach`). The spec's `dragTo` returned at mouse-up, so
the next click could land inside that window; the view stayed on the page editor, where
`useHeaderLabel` returns `''`.

The wait is now `dndClickGuardLifted(page)` in `e2e/helpers.ts`: a 50 ms timer started after the
release, so it fires after dnd-kit's. `dragTo` calls it, and so does `touchLongPress`, whose
hold-then-tap callers raced the same way. No assertion changed.

Decisions and traps:

- Latent since #6061, which gave `tab-reorder.spec.ts` this wait but not `page-link-drag`. Not a
  product bug: nobody clicks within 50 ms of a drop.
- To reproduce, stretch every 50 ms timer to 3 s from an init script: the old code failed eleven
  tests, the fixed code none. Stretching only dnd-kit's timer flags every spec that clicks soon
  after a drag.
- A lost click after a drag fails only when the next assertion depends on the view changing. In
  `bookmarked-pages.spec.ts` the `navigateToView` right after `dragBlock` can be swallowed and the
  test still passes, because it then reads only the sidebar. The mouse helper behind `dragBlock`
  was left alone; no caller went red.
- `tab-reorder.spec.ts` keeps its own copy of the wait.

Ran: the spec ten times under load, the nine spec files that use the changed helpers, and
`typecheck:e2e`. The whole Playwright lane is CI's run on this PR.

## Session 3971 — saving PDF annotations works, and keeps the block's link

`Refs #6629` (checkbox 1, the fix half; the highlight-block items wait on the design note there).

Two defects, one behind the other:

- Save in `PdfViewerDialog` had never enabled in the real app. pdf.js sends
  `editingstateschanged` with `isEmpty` under `details`; the listener read it at the top level.
  The unit test dispatched the top-level shape, which pdf.js never sends, so it passed. That one
  payload line in the existing test was corrected to the library's shape.
- With Save working, the annotated copy gets a new attachment id and the original is deleted, so
  an `attachment:<old id>` link in the owning block's text went dead. Save now rewrites those links
  through the page store before deleting the original, and keeps the original when the block's text
  cannot be read or the edit is refused: two files and a working link.

Decisions and traps:

- No backend command replaces an attachment's bytes under the same id, so the id has to change.
- The rewrite goes through the page store so a static block updates at once. Page mode would have
  recovered through its reload after `onSaved`; only the block-mode check made before navigating
  away shows the store route is needed.
- The text it rewrites is read from the backend, not the store: page mode saves through the block
  commands and leaves the store behind, so rewriting the store's copy wrote back over text typed in
  page mode since it opened. Review caught it; the page-mode spec now types first.
- The dialog takes its page store with `usePageBlockStoreApi`: both hosts (`StaticBlock`, page
  mode's `PageBlockMetadata`) render under one, so the no-store branch was test-only and is gone;
  the dialog's existing tests now render under a store too.
- Links to the old id in other blocks are not rewritten: nothing queries block text for attachment
  links. Documented as a limit.
- The rewrite is its own undo entry, so Ctrl+Z after a save restores a link to the deleted
  original. Attachment add and delete return no op refs to group with it.
- An event mock should copy the library's real payload.

Ran: full vitest, typecheck, lint, the prek hooks, and the Playwright specs selected for the diff.

## Session 3955 — bookmarks as a synced page property

`Refs #6544` (eighth checkbox). Design note and the maintainer's decisions are in the issue's
comments.

## Session 3931 — In-page replace: one or all matches in the open page (#6608)

The find bar gains a replace row (its toggle, or `Ctrl+H`): **Replace** takes the current match and moves past the text it put in, **Replace all** takes every match in the page, rows not mounted included.

**Block mode.** A replace rewrites the block's stored Markdown, not the rendered text. `replace.ts` parses the block, replaces in its text nodes, and serialises it again, so no rendered-text offset is mapped back into the Markdown (the issue's experiment). The find text walker in `block-text.ts` now also records which piece of the document each stretch came from (`findTextPieces`). A match is replaced only when every piece it covers is a text node. A chip label, a line break, the gap between two paragraphs, or an empty piece such as inline math makes the match skipped, and the row counts skipped matches. Overlapping matches keep the first. Capture groups expand natively: a sticky copy of the pattern runs at the match's offset and goes through `String.replace`.

The save goes through the page store's `edit`, after `flushActiveDraft()`. Once the save succeeds, `takeReloadedText` hands the new text to a roving editor still on the block; handing it over first left a refused replacement in the editor, committed by the next keystroke (review). For Replace all to be one undo entry per page, `edit` takes an `undoGroup` key. The first edit of a run on a page is `merge: false` with the key, and later edits join it by key, whatever the time between them. No new op, IPC, or store was needed. A failed `edit` stops the run.

**Page mode.** Find walks page mode's editor as DOM, so a replace there is one ProseMirror transaction (`replace-in-editor.ts`): page mode saves it as typing, and one undo in the editor takes it back. A DOM match maps to the document with `posAtDOM`. Text in a chip, a widget, or outside the editor gets no span of positions, so that length check alone rejects it. Three other checks (`nodesBetween`, `textBetween`, `view.dom.contains`) were tried and deleted, because removing any one of them left every test green. After a page-mode replace the DOM walk has to be re-run by hand (`replaceRun`), because no store change starts it.

**Traps.** `Cmd+H` hides the app on macOS. The binding is `Ctrl + H`, and the matcher accepts the physical Control key. This is unverified on a Mac. A Replace all of more than `MAX_COALESCED_UNDO_REFS` ops splits into one undo entry per chunk. Disabling Replace all once no match is left dropped focus to the page, where Escape does nothing, so focus goes back to the replace field.

**Measured** (scratch vitest, not committed): `replaceAllInContent` over 500 realistic blocks took about 70 to 160 ms in jsdom on the shared 4-core box. A run's time is the sequential `edit_block` IPCs, not the rewrite.

**Checks run:** vitest on `src/lib/in-page-find`, `InPageFind.test.tsx`, the new `InPageReplace.test.tsx`, `page-blocks.batch-op-refs.test.ts`, `useAppKeyboardShortcuts.test.ts`, and the keyboard-config suites. `npm run typecheck`. ## Session 3927 — A click into a block puts the caret where it landed (#6562, second checkbox)

**Goal.** A click or tap on a static block mounted the roving editor with the caret at the end, wherever the click was. #6599 stopped focusing a block from moving text, which this fix relies on: the click is read from the static render before the mount.

**What changed.**
- `src/editor/click-caret.ts` (new, TipTap-free at runtime): `textPointAtClick` reads the DOM point under the click (`caretPositionFromPoint`, or WebKit's `caretRangeFromPoint`) and `textPointAt` turns it into a `TextPoint`, a count of the document's own text characters plus a `bias`. A DOM text node counts when it continues the text runs of `parse(content)` (a highlighted code block splits one run over several nodes) or is a whole later run (a diagram's source is never drawn); chip labels, the list marker and the space the renderer puts between paragraphs count nothing, and whitespace alone never opens a run (review: the paragraph separator, or the space after a link, was taken for the next run's leading space, and every click after a link or in a later paragraph opening with a chip fell back to the end). Chips and block boundaries carry no characters, so `bias` picks the side: `'after'` at a run's start, `'before'` elsewhere, and for a point in uncounted text, after it unless the point is at its start. `posAtTextPoint` maps the point into the ProseMirror doc after the replace, clamping past the end to the end of the last textblock (after a trailing chip).
- `StaticBlock.handleOuterClick` passes the point to `onFocus`; `EditableBlock.handleFocus` hands it to `mount` as `cursorPlacement`; `mount` focuses at `posAtTextPoint`. `MountOptions.cursorPlacement` is now `'start' | 'end' | TextPoint`.
- A double-click needed nothing: its second press lands on the mounted editor, which selects the word natively (`sticky-toolbar.spec.ts` already pinned it).

## Session 3920 — A duplicate block from the mirror's choice of base text

#5363: main's CI failed `mirror_convergence::edits_from_both_sides_converge_with_no_duplicate_and_each_ingest_undoes` with a duplicated block. This session pins that case and fixes it.

### The cause

An ingest reads a file against the shadow (one of the last texts the mirror wrote for the page) that `closest` picks. `closest` counted blank lines. Every text shares blank lines, so a file line that only the newest text holds could tie with an older text. The tie went to the older text, which lacks that line, so `pair_unanchored_lines` paired the file's lines positionally against it. The line was renamed onto another block while its own block stayed: the same text in two blocks.

The case from CI: the shadows were `w0 w1 w2` and `w0 w1 w2 s0 s1`, and the file read `s7 s1 s8`. With blank lines counted, both shadows left three of the file's lines unexplained, and the older one won. Against it, `s1` was paired with `w1` while the `s1` block stayed.

### The change

- `closest` (`src/mirror/ingest.rs`) compares non-blank lines only. The newest text explains `s1` and wins.
- `docs/architecture/data-and-events.md` (*Ingest*) says so.

### Sibling paths

- `create` reads a new page's file against an empty buffer and makes no choice of shadow.
- `renamed_from` matches by anchors or exact bytes.
- The read-back confirmation goes through the same `ingest`, so the fix covers it.

### Tests

## Session 3905 — Auto-purge keeps a trash cohort that holds a block created after the delete

#6543: device A deletes page P; device B, not yet synced, writes C under P. After the exchange C sits in P's trash cohort on both devices, dated at A's delete, and `tombstone_purge` hard-deleted it with the cohort 90 days after that date, possibly days after C arrived. The two-device test reproduced this before the fix: C took P's `deleted_at` on both sides and the purge took P, its old child and C.

`tombstone_purge`'s candidate query now leaves out:
- every row sharing `deleted_at` with a "late" block, meaning a deleted block whose ULID prefix (the creation ms) is newer than its own `deleted_at`. This keeps the whole cohort, the late block included.
- every ancestor of a late block, because purge cascades down the subtree whatever the descendants' `deleted_at`. This covers a late block in an older nested cohort under a page deleted later.

The change only removes candidates, so auto-purge never deletes more than before. The ULID floor for a `deleted_at` is computed in SQL (Crockford base32 of the ms). Ids that are not 26 characters long carry no creation time and are never late. The existing purge tests seed ids like `AGED0001`, and without that clause five of them go red.

`tombstone_purge_before(cutoff_ms)` is the seam the two-device test uses to put the clock past the window. Back-dating `deleted_at` would make every block look late, because real commands mint ULIDs at the current time.

The issue placed the test in `agaric-sync`'s `loro_sync_tests.rs`. `tombstone_purge` lives in the app crate, which that crate cannot reach, so both tests are in `src/maintenance.rs`.

Not covered: an edit to a block that existed before the delete, a purge that already ran before the sync, and a writer whose clock runs behind the deleter's, so the late block's ULID predates the delete.

The `.sqlx` cache is regenerated for the new `query!`.

`purge_candidates_are_a_subset_of_the_pre_6543_query_and_late_test_is_exact` keeps the old query as an oracle: new is a subset of old, equal when no block is late, and the late test flips exactly one ms after `deleted_at`.

Ran: targeted nextest on the purge tests, red first, then each new clause falsified; `cargo check`, clippy and fmt across the workspace.
