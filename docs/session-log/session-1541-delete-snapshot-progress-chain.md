# Session 1541 — The snapshot progress bar nothing could ever fill

First PR of the afternoon batch run alongside a second agent in another container. The maintainer withdrew the #3487 "`MAX_SNAPSHOT_SIZE` stays" constraint that had held #4692 open, and a read-only scout then found that #4699 had already deleted everything the issue originally named: the constant, `CatchupOutcome::Rejected`, the `SnapshotAccept` / `SnapshotReject` wire variants and the test whose only subject was a constant nothing read. What #4699 did not touch is the chain the issue's later comments added: `SyncEvent::SnapshotProgress` and the `SyncProgressUpdate::Snapshot` payload it mapped to, both routed through the sinks and pinned by a test, and constructed by nothing in production since the iroh cutover closed the CBOR path. On the frontend that meant a mounted `SnapshotTransferProgress` panel in the status view that rendered null on every sync, three store fields with two actions nobody but their tests called, a `kind === 'snapshot'` branch in the sync trigger, and five i18n keys.

All of it is gone: the two Rust variants, the three sink arms and their test, the panel and its test, the store fields and actions, the trigger branch, the keys, and a filter arm in the catch-up test whose doc comment claimed an emission that did not exist. `bindings.ts` was regenerated; `SyncProgressUpdate` is a two-member union now. `ChannelEventSink::on_sync_event` dropped under the 70-line ceiling, so its `too_many_lines` expectation went with it. Three comments that narrated the deleted CBOR path were cut to their one live reason; the reviewer found the `#2538` note had landed on the success arm while describing the failure arm four lines down, which already carried its own version, and folded the two into one on the arm it describes. `transport::session::MAX_FRAME_SIZE` was confirmed to bound every inbound Loro catch-up frame before the older cap's last mention went, so nothing here needed a replacement guard.

The shared Rust target directory bit twice: cargo hashes workspace members by their path relative to the workspace root, so two worktrees produce identically named artifacts and one checkout's rlib can be taken as fresh by another. Both builder and reviewer forced a rebuild from this tree before trusting a run, and the reviewer verified the tested `agaric_sync` binary carried only this worktree's source paths and no `SnapshotProgress` symbol.
