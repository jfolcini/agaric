# Sync

Agaric syncs across your own devices over local WiFi. **No cloud, no accounts, and no third-party servers unless you turn on the internet fallback** (below). Edits converge automatically via CRDT — there is no conflict dialog because there are no merge conflicts to resolve.

## What you can do

- **Pair a new device** via QR code (scan with the new device's camera) or by typing a 4-word passphrase.
- **See your paired devices** in Settings → Sync & devices (the **DeviceManagement** tab).
- **Manually trigger a sync** from the sidebar's Sync button.
- **Cancel a running sync** from the device's row in Settings → Sync & devices: *Cancel sync* shows while that device's sync runs, from the moment it starts dialling, and ends the sync while it dials or waits for the other device's next message, even if that device has not answered. A cancelled sync reads as idle, with no failure toast, and costs the device no retry backoff (#5739). The cancel is device-wide: it stops every sync running on this device, one the other device started included, not only the one on that row. Changes already being applied, and a snapshot catch-up, finish first.
- **Unpair** a device (with confirmation).
- **Rename a device** so it's recognisable in your list.
- **Set a manual address** for an already-paired peer on your subnet, when mDNS discovery is unavailable. This does not help a device you have not paired yet, and does not reach across subnets — see the pitfalls below.
- **Turn on the internet fallback** (Settings → Sync & devices, *Local network, with an internet fallback*; off by default) when your devices can see each other but every connection dies — the VPN case in the pitfalls below. With it on, on **both** devices, sync goes through a connection server on the internet (a relay run by iroh's maintainers) whenever the direct path fails; the local network is still used whenever it works. The relay forwards encrypted traffic it cannot read, but it does see which of your devices are talking, from which addresses, when, and how much moves — and the device holds a connection to it for as long as sync is running. It takes effect the next time Agaric starts. It also changes what happens to a paired device that is away: once two devices have synced, each remembers the other's key and last address, and while the setting is on, every periodic sync dials a device the local network no longer sees at that last address *and* through the relay, paced by the usual retry backoff. Whether that sync completes with the two devices on different networks has not been tried yet (#6519); a first pair still needs both devices on the same network (#4549, #6564).

## Pairing flow

1. On the **first device**: Settings → Sync & devices → *Pair new device*. The **PairingDialog** opens with a QR code and a 4-word passphrase. The session lasts 5 minutes (the countdown pauses while you're typing on the other side).
2. On the **second device**: same path, then *Have a code from the other device?*. Choose *Scan QR code* (camera) or *Type passphrase* (4 word boxes).
3. On scan / entry, each device proves it knows the passphrase and the two add each other to their peer lists. There is no certificate exchange: the connection itself authenticates each device's cryptographic key, and that key is remembered on first contact (TOFU) — subsequent connections must present the same one.
4. The first sync runs immediately and brings the whole vault over: each space arrives in one message rather than edit by edit. Later syncs send only what changed.

If the camera fails (no permission, no camera), the scanner says why and offers *Retry camera*; *Type passphrase* stays available.

If you click the Sync button before any device is paired, the **NoPeersDialog** appears; its *Pair a device* opens the pairing dialog itself, closing the phone's navigation drawer first.

## What the user sees

- **Sidebar Sync icon** (footer), the one place the status dot is drawn:
  - Green: idle.
  - Spinning + amber: syncing now.
  - Red, with an alert glyph and the error in the tooltip: last attempt failed (toast offers *Retry*).
  - Grey, with *Waiting for Pixel 8, last synced 2h ago* in the tooltip: the other device is away. It neither announces itself on the network nor answers, and has not synced with this device lately; nothing failed, so there is no toast (#6523). A device that has only just left still reads as a failed sync until its last announcement on the network goes stale, a few minutes later.
  - Yellow, with *No devices paired* in the tooltip: nothing to sync with yet.
  - Strikethrough WiFi: offline.
- **"Last synced N ago"** in the Sync icon's tooltip, with the state. While waiting for an away device, the waiting line takes its place, with that device's own last sync.
- **Settings › Sync & devices**, at the top (below any sync changes that could not be applied) once a device is paired: the overall sync state, the last successful sync, and the peer count; while waiting, the device waited for. To retry, use the device list's *Sync all* or a device's *Sync now*.
- **Different versions of Agaric** do not sync. Both devices say why in the Sync & devices card and a toast, and which one to update: *Pixel 8 runs an older version of Agaric. Update Agaric on Pixel 8, then sync again.* On Android the toast links the releases page when the phone is the one to update. Paired with a device on a release from before #6523, the older device says to update both when the newer one dials it, and shows the newer device's English sentence telling it to update itself when it dials; the newer device names the older one when dialled, and says the sync could not start and, if that repeats, to check that both run the same version when it dials the older one, which ends the sync without answering. A device on the same version that quits or drops off the network at that moment gets the same sentence, which is why it only suggests the check (#5728, #6523).
- **Per-peer progress** in the device's row while a sync is running: sync messages sent and received, then the attachment transfer with files and bytes done of total and a progress bar; the row clears when the sync ends (#5842). It shows for syncs this device starts (*Sync now*, *Sync all*, and its own periodic sync), not for a session the other device dialled in. While it shows, the row offers *Cancel sync* (#5739).
- **Retry toast** per peer on failure — partial failures don't blow up the whole batch.
- **A space whose changes are held back** is named in a warning in the Sync & devices card and in Settings › App health: *Changes in the space Work are not reaching your other devices: its sync data and its database disagree about a deleted block.* When a block is deleted in the database and still live in the sync data, Agaric writes the deletion into the sync data as it sends the space, so the other device receives the delete (#6565). It holds the space back, and shows the warning, only when it cannot do that safely: a change from another device for that space is still waiting to be applied, or is listed under *Sync changes that could not be applied*. Every other space still syncs. The warning goes once the space is sent again; if it stays, restart Agaric, and *Retry* any change for that space in that list (#6523).
- **Sync changes that could not be applied**, first in Settings › Sync & devices, shown whether or not a device is paired and hidden when there are none (#6057): each row names the space, when the change first arrived, its size, and why it would not merge. *Retry* takes a change off the list and tries it again the next time Agaric starts, not at the next sync; its toast offers *Restart now*. A retry that still fails comes back to the list after that start, with the new reason.
- **Quarantine warning** at startup, only on the start that moved changes from another device to quarantine because they still could not be applied after repeated restarts: it says how many and stays until dismissed. Its *Review* opens Settings › Sync & devices. Later starts stay quiet, since a quarantined change cannot be deleted (#6057).

## How sync runs

- **Discovery**: mDNS announces and discovers peers on the local network. On Android, Agaric holds a multicast lock, without which the phone receives no mDNS replies; with the screen off or the app in the background Android drops the app's traffic, so discovery and sync resume when Agaric is back in front.
- **Automatic triggers**: an edit starts a sync about two seconds after the typing stops, and the other device, if it is running, then pulls the edit; the daemon also runs a periodic resync tick. Both back off exponentially on failure, per peer, with jitter so two devices don't lock-step.
- **Network changes**: when the device moves to another network, its network comes up after Agaric started, or its connection drops and comes back, sync picks the new connection up without a restart, at the next periodic check: about half a minute at most (#6526).
- **Offline / foreground awareness**: a device that goes offline stops trying immediately; a device that backgrounds skips its resync ticks until foreground. Coming back online retries immediately.
- **Wire**: QUIC (over UDP), encrypted with TLS 1.3. CRDT operations, snapshots and attachment bytes all flow over the same connection.
- **Blocks you added under a page another device purged** (#6561): if another device deletes a page, or a block with others under it, and empties its Trash while this one, out of contact, adds blocks under it, the next sync removes the page here and keeps what you added: this device puts those blocks, unchanged, in the Trash under a new page titled as the purged page was (for a purged parent block, its first line), and a toast that stays until you dismiss it says *Another device deleted or moved “Trip notes”. The blocks you added under it here that had not synced yet are in the Trash.* Restoring the page brings the blocks back. The other device gets the page in its Trash at its next sync. A page moved to another space leaves its old space the same way, so blocks added under it meanwhile land in the old space's Trash. Only blocks created here that the purging device had never received are kept: a block it had received (also one you had indented under a new block), a block only moved under the page, an empty block, and an offline edit to a block it already had are purged with the page. The kept blocks are saved before they can sync. A rescue that happens while the app recovers from a crash shows no toast.
- **Same names from two devices** (#6278): two devices that each made a page with one name in a space while apart (`Foo` and `foo` are one name) keep both pages once they sync. The device that made the newer page renames it to the first free `Foo (2)`, free among the space's titles and aliases, says so in a toast that stays until you dismiss it, and History lists the rename; the other device gets it at its next sync. Only that device renames, since two renames would merge into `Foo (2) (2)`. Two tags with one name merge into the older tag: the device that made the newer one moves its chips and its inline `#` references to the older tag and deletes the newer, unless a block of another space holds or names the newer, which leaves both as they are. Two pages for one journal day merge into the older page. A device that is gone leaves its newer page or tag as it is, and the older one holds the name everywhere. The same repair runs at each startup, for a pair a vault already holds; a startup rename shows no toast, since it runs before the window opens.

## Snapshot catch-up

When the changes one device would send cannot be applied on top of what the other holds, the sync switches to a full snapshot of each space. A fresh device never needs this: its first sync is the ordinary one above. Snapshots stream as raw bytes on the same connection and apply atomically, so a snapshot apply can never half-fail. It is a *merge*, not a wipe: anything you wrote on the catching-up device that the other side had not seen yet survives.

A snapshot cannot heal one case: a device that has lost changes *it made itself* while the other device still holds them. That is what restoring an older copy of a device's own database leads to. Agaric cannot tell the lost changes from whatever that device writes next in their place, so the snapshot is turned away, and every sync from that device fails the same way; it says why and what to do (see [Pitfalls](#pitfalls-to-know)).

## Per-peer settings

In **DeviceManagement** you can per-peer:

- **Rename** the device (the name shows up in tooltips, sync progress, the activity feed).
- **Set a manual address** (`host:port`) for an already-paired peer on your subnet that mDNS hasn't surfaced (e.g. it hasn't re-announced since a restart). The address to enter is the one the other device shows as *This device's address*, at the foot of its Settings → Sync & devices; the port stays the same across its restarts (#6526). It is not a way to reach another network — see the pitfalls below.
- **View status** (last sync, last error).
- **Unpair** — confirmation required (non-reversible without re-pairing).

## What's not synced

- **Local-only state**: keyboard customisations, sidebar width, recent tabs, last-opened view per space, theme preference, draft autosave entries that never persisted.
- **Link preview cache** (each device fetches independently).
- **MCP / agent activity feed** (per device).

## Pitfalls to know

- **Both devices must be on the same local network — always, and a first pair by passphrase also needs it to carry multicast.** Agaric never discovers devices over the internet (with the internet fallback on, a device you have already synced with is dialled through the relay by the key and address it remembers, even when mDNS no longer sees it; whether that reaches a device on another network is untested, #6519). A passphrase pair learns how to dial the other device from mDNS alone. A scanned QR code also carries the host's address (#4037), so a QR pair can complete where multicast is dropped (say, multicast disabled on the router) as long as the two devices can still reach each other directly. Where they can't (two subnets, a guest WiFi that isolates its clients) there's no way to complete a first pair — not even by typing an address, since the manual-address field only appears for a peer you've already paired.
- **A VPN can swallow the LAN even when both devices are on the same WiFi.** If the tunnel is not bypassable, whether your LAN escapes it depends on the address range the router hands out: a LAN numbered out of *public* address space is treated as internet-bound and routed into the tunnel. That range can look local without being private — `192.160.x.x` is one digit from `192.168.x.x` and is public. Link-local multicast (`224.0.0.251`, which mDNS uses) still escapes, which makes the symptom distinctive and misleading: **discovery works and every connection attempt dies** — the other device appears in your list and no sync ever starts. Turning the VPN off is the test. **Check every device, not just the desktop** — in the one recorded case the capturing tunnel was an enterprise profile on the Android phone, and the desktop was fine. Note also that this symptom has had at least one other cause: before #3853 a desktop bound its Docker bridge and advertised that over mDNS, which looks identical from the outside. **Since #4299 the app names this case itself**: when a dial times out against a peer mDNS is still announcing, Agaric asks the operating system which *source address* it would actually send that peer's traffic from, and if that address falls outside the subnet the sync endpoint bound — outside it, not merely different from it, so a second address on your own LAN (a `br0` bridge, a docked laptop's other NIC) is never mistaken for a tunnel — the peer's failure reads *"discovered on the network, but traffic to this device is being routed elsewhere - a VPN or firewall may be capturing your LAN"* instead of a bare timeout. The measurement is about **this** device's own routing, so the message appears on the captured device — the other end still shows a bare timeout, which is one more reason to check every device. It is a diagnosis, not a fix — the sync still does not happen until the capture is lifted or the **internet fallback** is turned on, on both devices (see *What you can do*): the relay leg is an outbound HTTPS connection that the tunnel routes perfectly well, which is exactly the case it exists for — and it deliberately says *routed elsewhere* rather than *VPN*, because a split-tunnel client, a corporate agent, a mis-scoped default route and a second link on a *different* subnet all produce the same measurement.
- **The manual address is not an across-network route.** Once paired, a peer's row in Settings → Sync & devices gains a manual `host:port` field, but it only helps for a peer on the same subnet Agaric bound — typically one that hasn't re-announced over mDNS yet. The sync endpoint is deliberately LAN-confined: it binds a single subnet as a non-default route, so a destination outside that subnet has no socket to leave by and the dial cannot be attempted at all. A VPN- or internet-routed address will not work. The internet fallback does not change this: the relay is a separate transport that rides the default route, and the bound socket stays confined.
- **First-launch firewall prompt** (especially on macOS): allow incoming connections so peers can reach Agaric. Agaric listens on **UDP**, on a port the operating system picked the first time and Agaric asks for again at each launch (#6526); Settings → Sync & devices shows it as *This device's address*. A hand-written rule can allow the application or that UDP port, not a TCP port. On Linux there is no prompt at all: a `ufw` or firewalld default-deny policy drops the inbound half silently. A rule can name the port (`ufw allow in on <iface> proto udp from <subnet> to any port <port>`), but the port moves if another program holds it when Agaric starts, so the rule that keeps working names the interface and protocol: `ufw allow in on <iface> proto udp from <subnet>` — for example `from 192.168.1.0/24`. Scope it to the **subnet**, not to a single peer address: a default-deny policy drops inbound mDNS too, so the machine that needs this rule is precisely the one whose peer never appeared in the device list to have its address read off. A single-host form (`from <peer-ip>`) is tighter once you do know the address, but it is pinned to that peer's current DHCP lease, so a lease rotation silently reinstates the drop unless you also hold a DHCP reservation. A rule without a port grants more than Agaric: it opens **every** UDP port on that interface to the permitted source.
- **mDNS on Android needs multicast — built in.** Some routers disable multicast; if your other devices can see each other on the network but Agaric can't, that's the likely cause.
- **TOFU on first pair.** If you re-install Agaric on a peer and its app data is wiped, the device comes back with a different cryptographic identity — you'll need to unpair and re-pair.
- **A restored copy of another device's app data *is* that device, and will not pair with it.** The app data directory holds `device-id` and `sync-endpoint.key` next to the vault, so restoring a backup onto a second install brings the first device's identity with it. That identity is not decoration: every operation in the log is hashed under its `device_id` and keyed by `(device_id, sequence number)`, so two installs answering to one id would hand different edits the same slot. Agaric refuses the pair instead: the receiving device answers *"cannot sync with self"* and says nothing to its own user (there is nothing that user did, or can do, about it), and the device that dialled only sees the sync fail — so the symptom is a pair that never completes, right after a restore. **Copying the app data directory is not how you set up a second device**: install Agaric there and pair, and the first sync fills the vault for you. A new phone is set up the same way: Agaric opts out of Android's backup and phone-to-phone transfer, so a phone never starts as a restored copy (#6538). If you already have such a copy and have **not** edited it since the restore, quit it, delete `device-id` **and** `sync-endpoint.key` from its app data directory, and start it again — it mints a fresh identity, leaves your notes alone, and can then be paired like any new device. If you *have* edited it, that no longer works and you must not try it: a new identity only changes who authors ops from now on, while the edits both copies made since the restore are already written under the original device's identity, in the same slots. The pair then completes and every sync after it fails permanently — the merge asks for a snapshot, and the snapshot cannot heal a device that forked its own identity space (#792, #2503). Export those notes out of the copy instead (per-page export or export-all-as-ZIP), then set the device up fresh and import them.
- **A device restored from an older copy of its own database stops syncing.** The other device still holds changes this device made after that copy was taken, and whatever this device writes next takes their place, so the two cannot be merged: every sync from the restored device fails, and the failure reads *Pixel 8 holds changes this device made and no longer has, as after restoring an older backup here, so the two cannot merge. Export the notes from this device, set Agaric up fresh on it, pair it again, then import them.* in the Sync & devices card and, once per run of failures, a toast (#6523). Do that: export what the restored device holds (per-page export or export-all-as-ZIP), set it up fresh, pair it, and import. Agaric does not repair this by itself (#792, #2503).
- **Upgrading from a pre-QUIC build re-establishes trust once.** Existing pairings survive the upgrade, but the old certificate could not be converted into the new key, so each pair silently re-learns the other's identity on its first sync after the update. Do the first post-upgrade sync on a network you trust.
- **A desktop on both WiFi and Ethernet only accepts on one of them.** If two devices are on the same LAN but only reachable across different interfaces, they may show up in discovery and still fail to connect. Agaric prefers a physical interface over a cellular or virtual one, and between two of the same kind it prefers the one carrying your default route — so it is usually the interface your other internet traffic already uses. Unplugging the one you do *not* want it on is the reliable way to force the choice.
- **Pairing session is 5 minutes.** If the timer expires, restart from the first device.
- **A press on Sync dials at once.** The sidebar button, *Sync now*, *Sync all* and a failure toast's *Retry* dial even a device that a failed sync left waiting for its next retry, so the press reports that device's real result; the automatic sync leaves such a device until its retry is due (#6523). If you've just edited something, the auto-sync may have already fired by the time you click.
- **Unpairing only takes effect on the device you do it from.** There is no message telling the other device it has been unpaired — that device keeps its half of the pairing and keeps trying to sync, and every attempt is refused. It notices on its own within a minute or two and its entry for you turns into a **Pairing lost** state that says to pair again; until then it may still show a recent sync time. To be rid of a pairing on both sides, unpair on both, or pair again to revive it.
- **A change that will not merge is kept, not dropped.** An incoming change that still has not applied after `QUARANTINE_AFTER_BOOTS` restarts, because its payload is truncated or corrupt or it waits on a change its sender never delivers, stops being retried and is listed under *Sync changes that could not be applied*. Its bytes stay on the device, but its content is not in your notes, and unpairing does not clear it. *Retry* gets it one more start: there it applies, comes back to the list, or, if this device can no longer reach the change it was built on, is dropped, and the next sync with its sender heals the space by snapshot catch-up.
- **Unpaired by mistake?** Pair again from either device. The new pairing replaces the previously remembered identity via TOFU, and clears the *Pairing lost* state on the device that was left behind.
